*This was all far, far worse than Misa had imagined.*

*If she had known from the start how deep the hole would go, would she have still plunged in? She hoped so, but perhaps she would have brought a longer rope.*

*It had started with the supposed death of Misa's friend, comrade, and former pupil Hermina Green from critical soul gem exhaustion. Her death had occurred on* HSS Charles Drew, *a droneship dedicated to ferrying disconnected soul gems back from the front for reconstitution in new bodies. The situation had been chaotic, and the single specialty mage aboard the ship had not been able to stabilize her gem, which had gone critical unexpectedly.*

*That was the official story, and it had smelled wrong from the start. Misa had been around the block with soul gems in various states of fitness, becoming far more of an expert than she'd like. Hermina had been exhausted, but fine, having burned most of her remaining magic in a low‐orbit maneuver that compelled her to eject her gem away from a body that was going to tumble through an alien minefield. Twirling a set of cutting lasers that had grown nearly a kilometer long, she had been a goddamn hero, salvaging their escape from a convoy raid gone wrong.*

*Misa had been given a chance to inspect the gem herself, as it rocketed by in the arms of a support drone. With only a few grief cubes and some stabilization magic, she should have survived—valiant heroics were a good prophylactic against grief spirals.*

*Which was why the death notice had come as such a shock. After a few days, numb disbelief had given way to quiet suspicion. The whole thing felt ever so slightly* off.

*She had known better than to ask Governance and its imperturbably pristine networks. If something untoward had truly occurred, it had to be with Governance's cooperation—or involve magic. She would never get any answer and only possibly tip someone off.*

*To Misa's instincts, the whole thing felt like some kind of Internal Security operation, the kind you would pull if you needed magical girls for something completely black. Misa had sorted the possibilities in her head:*

*One, and most likely, she was completely off her rocker, in denial about her friend's death and getting drawn to conspiratorial thinking like to a lightning rod. She could picture the look on her MHD psychiatrist's face pretty well, if she voiced her concerns.*

*Two, a Governance or MSY cell had kidnapped Hermina for its own purposes. And it had to be "kidnapped"—there was no way she had volunteered. Misa had heard rumors among MagOps veterans about mysterious deaths and disappearances—some feared that Governance was creating their own magical girl organization, while others posited that the MSY, now public, was secretly forming a second, inner organization that could operate more independently, perhaps led by a not‐really‐so‐dead Homura.*

*Misa knew just enough about Internal Security to conclude that the latter was rather more likely than the former, and had* just *enough independence to want to snoop for herself. Risky, yes, but she had seen enough in her own experience, particularly with rogue colonies, to know that Hermina might not approve of her new mission. And Hermina had been her friend and comrade. That mattered.*

*Three… well, three was the worst possibility, because it would be something she couldn't quite imagine. She hadn't been able to shake that possibility out of her mind, the vague sense that she was seeing traces of the outline of… something.*

*Delusion, or intuition? She knew what Nadya would say.*

*With almost nothing else to go on, she had decided to investigate the mage responsible for managing the soul gems.*

*Even* that *had required a good deal of effort. Even with her MagOps access, she wouldn't have been able to access something as private as the military travel logs of a total stranger. She'd had to call in a few favors, and eventually an electronics specialist she had once courted got her a look, though her ex had insisted on telepathic verification that she wasn't lying. Honestly, fair enough.*

*A zap of magic later, and she had them, the completely mundane military history of a magical girl who spent most of her time on various soul gem shuttles, cycling back and forth between risky front‐line patrols and sedate body attachment assignments in the rear. There seemed to be nothing to go on at first, but then another zap elevated her classification privileges, and she found that the woman had a history of inquiries into her activity by various administrative AIs.*

*It was dull material by normal standards, AIs following up on some kind of complaint, followed by a perfunctory "No finding of misconduct". The kind of thing the individual herself would never be informed about, it was so minor. But once Misa read into the substance of the complaints, she lost the dull apathy that had begun to settle over her.*

*There was a* pattern *of complaints, all similar to Misa's own situation. Not directed at the magical girl herself, but regarding various soul gems that had passed through her care. The administrative AIs had passed by, audited the situation and her role in it, and found nothing out of line.*

*The pattern, though subtle, was striking, of the kind that an AI audit should perhaps have noticed.*

*It was Misa's own TacComp that noticed the next discrepancy.*

*You see, one of the records stated that the soul mage had been on New California taking a long‐awaited leave, something corroborated by a number of surveillance reports about her whereabouts on planet. Except one of the complaints clearly listed a date* within *that leave window as when she had been responsible for a lost soul gem, and that was* also *corroborated by military records, which furthermore didn't even mention her going on leave.*

*The discrepancy was blatant once you noticed the date overlap, and crosschecks with other records failed to resolve it as some kind of bookkeeping error, instead buttressing both sets of dates. And the most natural follow‐up—inspecting surrounding details to try to discern which set of facts was false—led Misa directly to side‐by‐side surveillance footage of what appeared to be the same magical girl in two different locations, doing exactly what the records said.*

*But how had any of this slipped past Governance's verification code? The answer was obvious: it hadn't. It had been noticed and ignored, and there were a number of possibilities as to why the discrepancy hadn't been properly concealed: for instance, an untrained magic user that had failed to hide her trail, or an AI that had wiped its own memory.*

*But one thing was immediately apparent: if she wanted any chance of chasing this lead, she needed to book her own trip to New California, and make sure no one knew why she was really there.*

*So she swore her electronics ex to secrecy, and booked her leave.*

---

*Misa had been around the colonies long enough to understand that there were plenty of unofficial channels for getting answers, even when—or perhaps especially when—Governance or the colonial government couldn't be relied upon. Outside of Earth, Governance was hardly omniscient, which left plenty of room for independent actors to find a niche, and for customers to develop a need.*

*Which was a long‐winded way of saying that there were freelance magical girls willing to use their magic privately for a bit of compensation, or just personal gratification. Misa had met one during one of her tours, a clairvoyant who went by Stacy—no surname at all, just Stacy—who styled herself as a private eye specializing in cold missing‐person‐or‐item cases. No one in the military really* needed *a side gig—their salaries were high partially to prevent this kind of thing—but she just liked doing it.*

*By a stroke of luck, Stacy was available, or available enough that she could file leave once Misa contacted her. Misa had plenty of leave, so it seemed wise to cover their tracks a little by having Stacy arrive a week later, after she had settled in a bit. Misa covered her own tracks by traveling with two of her squadmates and friends, Mina and Gracia, who both thought they were on a legitimate group vacation.*

*Paranoid? Maybe.*

*Misa and Stacy had finally met at a certain Café Liberica, where Misa outlined what she had found so far, cringing at times as she realized how circumstantial and conspiratorial it all sounded, the kind of thing that could have easily been written off as process errors.*

*Thankfully, Stacy agreed that there was at least something worth looking at, and that she had been the right person to call. After all, everything Misa had regarding New California—the discrepancies, when the soul mage had last been here—was at least a year in the past. But Stacy specialized in using high‐level sentimental, emotional, or even incidental ties—in particular, with a sentimental object—to look for connections deep in the past.*

*But Hermina, as Misa had pointed out, had allegedly died very recently. If they could find a trail to* her *here on New California, then not only would it be a very strong signal, it would prove something was up.*

*Their expectations of a brutally difficult search were then disproven by a simple visit to the starport, where Stacy picked up the trail almost immediately. They agreed that this was confusing: surely any conspiracy of this sort would have been capable of avoiding civilian logistics hubs.*

*Regardless of the reason, it gave them an easy trail to follow. Whoever it was* had *taken some measures to magically screen themselves, but not enough—though this implied little. Few magical girls were capable of effectively hiding* every *possible trail, and the MSY tried to keep tabs on those who could; simply looking up* that *bit of information in the power development archives had been an effort in its own right.*

*Confusingly, according to Stacy, Hermina had been reunited with her body, either her original or a clone, even if she wasn't conscious for the journey. Both options suggested Governance involvement, which would certainly explain why Governance AIs had apparently been overlooking all this.*

*At this point, she and Stacy had seriously discussed bringing the matter up to contacts in the Soul Guard. They held back only because, given there had to be magical girls involved as well, it was probably a joint Governance‐MSY operation, and in that case contacting the MSY wouldn't help.*

*They agreed to push onward, both of them priming pre‐existing dead man's switches to tell someone they trusted about what had happened, just in case.*

*Misa's, of course, was pointed at Nadya Antipova. Of everyone she knew, Nadya would be the very last to give up on her.*

---

*The trail led well outside the city of Amador, past a thin strip of suburbs and into bucolic robotic ranchland, where weary genetically‐engineered sheep grazed on sparse vegetation, interspersed with a few locals who had chosen to try a rural life. The gravity of their situation was definitely starting to get to her; Misa couldn't shake the sense that she was being watched.*

*The trail led to a remote lab, registered on the map as an agricultural research facility.*

*Misa hadn't intended to get herself mired too deeply. The original goal was only to take a look and see if she could figure out what was going on. If Hermina had been willingly recruited into some kind of Governance black op, then she was going to leave well enough alone and get out. If there was something more sinister going on, then even if it was a matter of saving a friend, she had to consider calling for help. Was it really wise for a magical girl, even of her skills, to take on a potentially hostile magical girl conspiracy alone?*

*But when she stepped inside the windowless two‐floor building—using a ceiling entrance—she found the security shockingly light, almost nonexistent, and though it was impossible to rule out stepping into a trap, what she saw soon blasted all sense of moderation out of her mind.*

*As she stepped through a threshold, a piece of her awareness snapped into focus, a piece she hadn't even been aware was missing.*

*There had been some security after all, the lightest of magical screens, blocking her perception of what was within.*

*But even as she struggled with the implications of that, instantly on her guard against potential enemy magic, she couldn't ignore what she now sensed:*

*Soul gems, perhaps seven of them, arrayed in a neat row a dozen meters away from her. Not active, and not in good state. Far from it; all her instincts screamed at her to get moving, that there were people in need of* saving.

*It took all of her paranoia to stay cautious, taking a moment to send a telepathic message to Stacy and to scan her surroundings for anything out of the ordinary. A magical perception barrier didn't just exist in a vacuum—something, or* someone *was powering it, and it couldn't last very long away from a magical girl either way.*

*But if there were soul gems on the brink of despair, then she needed to move to save them, even if it meant recklessly confronting an enemy.*

*A quick zap, and the electronic lock on the door in front of her gave way. The hallway behind had the air of a hospital, room after room of medical equipment spaced evenly apart, though only a few were occupied, and the walls between rooms were clear glass—hardly the practice of any actual hospital.*

*That clear glass also meant that Misa could take in everything with a glance, magically augmented vision picking out both details and gestalt. The soul gems at the end of the hallway were in one room each, placed on a small table within some kind of metal apparatus which she couldn't identify at this range. Next to each table was a cot with a seemingly unconscious girl on it, hooked up to medical equipment.*

*She felt the bile rise in her throat as she edged forward. This was wrong. Whatever it was, this was wrong.*

*She felt something else as she got closer: grief cubes, almost unused, concentrated around each soul gem. That was sensible enough, but then why were the soul gems so depleted?*

*Only when she got close did she understand: the grief cubes were just slightly, painfully out of reach of the gems, sealed into a glass container. A photometer similar to the ones they wore in combat sat nearby and, as she watched, one of the glass containers shifted a few centimeters toward its gem, just long enough to bring it slightly away from the brink. Then it withdrew.*

*Misa had heard of torture devices like this, but only in the least credible kind of Unification Wars shock schlock. It wasn't supposed to be practical—no information you extracted from a magical girl like this was reliable, and the kind of effort necessary to keep a magical girl confined like this was excessive, to put it mildly. At the end of the day, you were better off with a forced telepathic mind‐read—same amount of effort for much more reliable information, with much less patina of pure evil. Not even the Freedom Alliance had resisted such simple utilitarian logic.*

*That logic rang through her mind in a flash, in an aghast, almost delusional direct denial of what she was seeing in front of her.*

Why? How? *another part of her demanded.*

*How* were *these magical girls being kept confined? Had their jailor already fled? Now that she was closer, she realized what had been missing. There had been no telepathy, or any sense of emotion at all for what should have been the worst of situations. Now, though, she felt a distant, murmuring sense of…*

*There was no need to dig into that any further, even if she had wanted to. There were seven soul gems in her vicinity, all of them in torment. One of them was Hermina.*

*Getting them out was more important than anything.*

---

Kyouko had to stop there, finding herself running through the same logic, confusion, and disgust Misa had. It wasn't just evil, it was senseless.

Something had to be missing.

And the thought that this had been happening under their noses…

This had all happened over a decade ago, too. It ought to have *ended* a decade ago. Why hadn't Homura asked for more help?

---

*Misa was about to update Stacy when another sensation sent a shard of ice down her spine. There was someone else here. A magical girl other than her and the victims on their cots.*

*Despite everything around her, she felt the sudden, keen clarity of combat. She was no stealth specialist—a point of pure stubbornness on her part, she* could *have pursued power development into illusion magic, but no, she had always wanted more voltage, more current—and so she couldn't hope to sneak up on anyone who was actually using the building's surveillance systems. But she* was *suppressing her soul gem emissions, and whoever she was sensing didn't seem like they were being particularly careful, or were even aware anyone else was present.*

*Sometimes magical girls just forgot to use the mundane tools at their disposal, relying too much on their magic senses.*

*As carefully as she could, she signaled Stacy to join her at her mark. Misa was very confident in her skills in a close engagement, but two was still better than one, and clairvoyants had a lot of utility even if they never threw a punch. Besides, Stacy could phase through walls—she'd still have a good chance of escape if things went sideways.*

*Misa found her target in the next corridor over, facing the other way towards one of the patient rooms—if they could even be called that. They were seemingly oblivious, perhaps lost in thought. They weren't even transformed.*

"*Hold it right there,*" *Misa said, arm raised.* "*Keep your hands where I can see them. Transform and I drop you.*"

*She felt the comforting blip of Stacy's magic behind her, signaling she was about to arrive.*

*Now that there was no need to hide, Misa gathered power around her, enough voltage that the air crackled, channeling power into her own body, lifting herself slightly above the ground. She could fry anything nearby at a moment's notice, or pull a bullet out of the air. Many bullets, even.*

*The girl in front of her raised her hands slowly.*

*Did it really make sense to ask a magical girl to raise her hands? Kind of—many were unaccustomed to fighting without using their arms, and it was at least a signal of acquiescence. But Misa couldn't drop her guard.*

"*Turn around, slowly,*" *she said.*

"*I'm not your enemy,*" *the girl said, evenly, after she had turned and given Misa a good look at her face.*

"*Really? Then what the hell are you doing here, with all…* this?"

*Misa let her disgust show a bit. She wouldn't make any premature assumptions, but what else could possibly be going on?*

"*We're here to stop it,*" *the girl said.* "*We have nothing to do with what was going on here.*"

"*A likely story.*"

We? *Misa thought, simultaneously.*

*But even as she responded, she felt another clear surge of magic behind her.*

*Perhaps she should have fried the girl in front of her on the spot, but something made her hesitate. There was something—*

*She recognized the magic behind her, she realized. She had only felt it once, but that had been enough to sear it into her memory, ten years ago, at New Athens.*

---

*Akemi Homura was cordial, once she had convinced Misa and Stacy to stop holding the other girl at figurative gunpoint—though Misa stayed transformed and kept much of her voltage field up, especially after she got a good look at 'Homura'.*

*She didn't* look *like Homura, of course. This girl with dark skin and striking eyes rather looked like Misa herself, if she were honest. But the feeling of her magic was undeniable—assuming someone hadn't figured out how to fake it, of course. Nothing could be entirely ruled out.*

Inhabiting other bodies is one method to evade detection, *this Homura thought.* It's proven necessary. But let's end our business here quickly: I'd rather not leave the victims of this facility here any longer than we have to.

---

Kyouko stopped again, taking a moment to refocus. She wasn't sure why she felt so stunned—the séance she had attended with Mami had told her Homura was using other bodies, and they already knew about Valentin.

Perhaps it was simply the look on "Homura's" face. In the séance, Homura had appeared as her normal self, as a quirk of the magic. In Misa's memories, she was someone else. And not just physically… the telepathy itself was slightly off too, like it had traveled through some protective medium.

Kyouko replayed the memory. She didn't think she was just imagining it, even if the telepathic impression was second‐hand. Homura was protecting her emotional state—hardly an unreasonable action here—but there was something else going on. Homura was going out of her way to project an emotional persona. One of a detached Ancient who had everything under control.

That was interesting, because it strongly implied she *wasn't* that.

Ever since New Athens, Kyouko had been very worried about Homura's mental health. Like most in the Church, she had figured her lost to a deep despair, wandering space alone on some private quest… until recently, when it turned out that Homura seemed to not only be managing a new magical girl organization, but also the Prometheus Institute on Earth as Joanne Valentin. That tended to suggest she was keeping it together, to say the least.

She had looked into Valentin of course, along with everyone else. Valentin had run a successful research lab, socialized with her colleagues and subordinates—notably, Ryouko's parents—and even had time to raise new AIs like Vlad. If anything, her personality had been more upbeat and positive than Kyouko had ever known Homura to be.

For a time Kyouko had considered it likely that Homura was better off now—talking more with the Goddess, perhaps even enjoying her new roles in life. A bit depressing to contemplate as a friend, but Kyouko understood as well as anyone the merits of a good breakup.

Mami and Yuma had thought otherwise, of course, and now she was facing the other possibility: that these new bodies enabled Homura to retreat fully into her masking, in a way none of them would ever have let her get away with before. That was what she was getting from this memory.

Maybe she was reading into it too much.

---

I can agree with that much, *Misa thought.* But it will take more than that to convince me to drop my guard, or not call for backup.

*What they really needed was a solid telepathic read on either Homura or her companion, using Stacy's limited mind‐reading skills. But both of the magical girls in front of them kept their mental guards solidly up.*

The TCF is compromised, *Homura thought.* Our mutual enemies have been using that for years now to conceal their operations from Governance and the MSY. Without a doubt, if you contacted anyone, you'd make everything worse. We went to a lot of trouble to isolate this facility.

*Misa thought feverishly. She wanted to believe the girl in front of her, oh yes, but the consequences of being wrong were so high, and so sharp.*

*The girl that was Homura closed her eyes thoughtfully.*

I'll tell you some of what I know, *she said.* These people, this rogue conspiracy, has been around for a while, since well before the war, though I do not know how much longer. They are possessed of some kind of predictive engine—an impossibly‐effective algorithm, one partially magical. They're ideologues, pursuing radical visions of utopia. Magical research forms a substantial part of their operations. Research like what you see around you.

Research? This is utopian research? *Misa asked, appalled.*

Of a sort. Certainly not research the MSY would approve of. We think that's part of what this is all about. All this around you is an attempt to recreate the conditions I experienced on New Athens, to unlock that kind of magical energy. Foolishly, I might add. It would never work, but it was enough to force us to come and stop it.

---

Kyouko felt a slight shiver pass through her. Now that Homura had said it, it made sense. The Church viewed Homura's miracle on New Athens as just that—a miracle—but she could imagine someone of a different, more exploitative mindset seeing instead an avenue toward great power.

More than that, she recalled what Mami had told her and Yuma, about what she had experienced with Akari before the Goddess saved her soul—and what Homura had cryptically hinted at, over the centuries.

She could guess that Homura would feel compelled to intervene for reasons beyond simple mercy.

---

It took you too long, *Misa thought. She could still feel Hermina's soul, and the others, on the brink of despair. The stalemate was agonizing.*

Unfortunately, *Homura thought,* while their understanding of magic is limited by their misapprehension of the Law of Cycles, they are still quite capable—they have made breakthroughs, and they aren't easily suppressed.

How capable are we talking? *Misa thought.*

Despite all our secrecy measures, they've already identified me and many of my original operatives as their enemies, while we have failed to identify their leadership. Despite a casualty ratio lopsided in our favor, their organization is growing more quickly, and employing more bodies more effectively, per member. Despite nearly seven years of conflict, and more sacrifices than I care to think about, I am little closer to protecting Humanity from them.

*Misa pinged Stacy telepathically. What did she think of all this?*

I'm inclined to believe her, *Stacy thought.* I don't have a concrete reason; it just doesn't feel like she's lying, and I have some experience questioning people. But if anyone could lie successfully to my face…

Yeah, *Misa agreed.*

*The magical girl standing next to Homura, the one that she had originally accosted, shifted in place.*

Perhaps I can break the impasse, *she thought.* Akemi is reluctant to drop her mental guard, but I can. I've felt your attempts to probe me. You will see that I am not lying.

*It was a surprising offer, given the risk that Misa and Stacy themselves were malicious actors, but thus far Homura and her companion had seemed largely unconcerned with that possibility.*

*Perhaps it was simply that Homura didn't really fear them. After all, they had apparently been foolish enough to show up in their own, only bodies.*

Makes sense, *Stacy agreed tersely.*

*There was a tense silence as Misa waited for her friend to perform the mind‐read, trying to conceal her nervousness as she scanned her surroundings for any sign of trouble. It had occurred to her, of course, that Homura could simply be stalling for time for reinforcements.*

They seem to be telling the truth, *Stacy thought.* Or at least this girl is. Apparently they were watching us arrive and were initially curious if *we* were involved with this facility, until you went inside and it was obvious you weren't, given how much grief your soul gem immediately began accumulating.

*So Misa hadn't gotten the jump on them after all. Ultimately, not that surprising.*

*Misa relaxed slightly, releasing much of the voltage in the air and dropping back towards the floor. Stacy wasn't a very good telepath, but she couldn't justify drawing this out any further.*

*Not that she dropped her guard entirely. She even started applying a couple grief cubes surreptitiously, to recharge.*

So what is it that you want us to do? *Misa asked.* And even if you're trying to hide yourself, what good does it do not to call for backup? If we don't let the world know what happened here, aren't you doing the enemy's work for them?

A very good question, *Homura said.* And one I don't relish having to answer. But better than resorting to force.

*The girl paused for a moment, peering at Misa's face until she grew just a little uncomfortable.*

The enemy's penetration of Governance networks is deep. If you called for help here by mundane means, it would be intercepted, and you would become a target, *Homura thought.* That's one reason. More importantly, I'm unwilling to precipitate a crisis because of the third party involved here: the Cephalopods.

*Misa recoiled slightly.*

We've discovered, *Homura continued,* that these people are cooperating with the Cephalopods attacking Humanity—the aliens have even given some stealth technology to them in return. But we also know that they aren't wholeheartedly aiding the Cephalopods—we've seen them pass up easy opportunities to do so. At the least, they're certainly holding back their access to Governance military intel. If the whole weight of Governance and the MSY were to suddenly bear down upon them, they would be forced to flee into the aliens' arms, and to give them what they truly want… Humanity isn't ready for that. Not yet. I have put plans into motion, but they'll take years longer to mature.

*She peered at Misa for a moment longer.*

I know I'm speaking in riddles. The truth is I've been given the same riddles. I've been given the conclusions without the premises. That's how it is. But I mean what I say about the risks. We have been forced to keep everything out of the light.

*She turned her head slightly, so that Misa could see the red ribbon hidden behind her hair, twin to the one in the Cult headquarters. She held that pose just long enough for Misa to take the meaning.*

I do wish She'd be more open with me, though I suspect I know why She won't be. But, even so, I must continue to fight this battle. You don't need to. Frankly, I hate recruiting people into it, and we already have more than I'd like.

*Homura closed her eyes for a moment.*

I know that's a lot to take in. Under better circumstances, I'd appeal to your faith in the Goddess to let us walk away, but I have to ask for more than that. I have to ask for so much you'll certainly balk; I would in your place. So I'll have to offer you something else.

---

The next memory was tagged differently, and Kyouko realized with a start that it didn't have Misa Virani's metadata. Instead, the files were labeled as if they came from *Akemi Homura*.

"*Homura gave you memories?*" Kyouko asked, dumbfounded.

Van Rossum raised an eyebrow, suddenly miffed that Kyouko had gotten the memories first. Or jealous, Kyouko thought.

"*Then I blew up the facility myself to hide her tracks, and let them Reformat me to wipe it all away, even the feelings regarding Hermina's 'death' that led me there. So her memories were obviously pretty convincing.*"

She paused for a moment to make sure she had their attention.

"*I've reordered them to be chronological,*" Misa commented. "*So they'd form a cohesive narrative. Most of these I actually got later, either as part of a new recruit package or just recently. Homura believes in making sure we have all the necessary context, in a hard‐to‐fake way. I can't provide you the magic certs, so you'll have to take my word here, but they're real. May the Goddess strike me down where I stand if I'm lying.*"

"*Since when do* you *believe in the Goddess?*" Gracia asked. "*No, wait, that's obvious.*"

The man that was Misa's current body shook his head, laughing to himself.

"*You remember when I gave Mina a good zap, right after she made a vow like that? On Karnāta? Nevermind—Kyouko, you know Homura. You'll recognize that these memories are real. Just experience them.*"

---

The first tranche of memories dated as early as 2440, covering the only period where Internal Security's tracking efforts had met with any success. Even then, this was much more informative, of course—Kuroi Nana would have a keen professional interest in these memories, for the sake of validating the effectiveness of her methods if nothing else.

Homura had wandered the battlefields of the early war, across the Ganges Sector, infiltrating alien positions, monitoring activity and taking notes, and at times intervening from the edges of battles, a silent contributor to some early war defensive stands. No, Kyouko concluded. Homura had never rejected suffering. She just didn't want to share it with others.

It was more ineffable sentiment than concrete notion: the hesitation before healing an injured arm, l'appel du vide of joining an unwinnable battle in a destroyed city, the fatalistic welcome for a squadron of enemy fighters that broke off to chase only her, the disdainful roteness in executing a score of aliens downrange.

Were those real feelings, or was Kyouko imputing false emotion onto mere actions? She doubted herself, suddenly.

If they were, these weren't the actions of someone truly trying to protect Humanity. They were the grim battles of someone seeking a fight noble enough to justify their end.

They want to be seen, but not in any way that allows a target lock, and pull observers toward that planet, *Homura thought, seemingly to herself.* That's not a theory I would have ever come up with. I'll have to take your word on that.

I'm really going to have to take your word on that, *Homura thought.*

You know I don't doubt you on the details.

You say this is going to be important, but it's not going to be important enough, is it? Not anytime soon?

Never the straight answer. I'm glad it's that important, at least.

It seemed Homura was unconcerned about being detected by an alien warship, and nowhere in the memory was it clear why.

Protecting the world a little longer was the promise she had made the Goddess, and all she had done recently felt like puttering around, making small differences here and there. Kyouko frowned at that thought from Homura. How exactly was Homura expecting to go stand right in the middle of several magical girls and expect to be undetected? "*As discussed previously, artifacts and communications from other Thinker factions are to be handed over to us immediately without inspection. We are here to collect.*"

Thinker? Is that what they call themselves?

*The drone replied:*

"*We have protested before about your refusal to let us know basic facts of importance to us. Partners must operate under a spirit of basic cooperation.*"

They don't seem on happy terms, but *partners? Homura thought.* Other alien factions? My opinion of anyone who works together with the aliens is quite low, but if there is more than one alien faction, then perhaps there are some who can be negotiated with. Perhaps even these can be.

Alright we'll discuss plans afterward, *she thought.* Clearest answer you've given me yet. I suppose you brought me here to show me this, but couldn't this have happened earlier?

Naturally.

*More useful information. The organizational name didn't tell her much that she hadn't been told already, but this proved that the aliens had extensive dealings with them, and moreover* understood *them as a social construct. …and I don't know what I'm allowed to say, and it might sound bad. That *was another clear telepathic read. So much for the questioning.*

"Alright," *she thought finally.* "Please, let's both call our ships in, and I'll follow you in mine. I hope you'll understand if I want to travel by myself, and have eyes on you."

How big a danger are these people? Am I to focus on them, rather than the aliens and the war?

*The distinctive pause.*

Finally. This feels like real progress. I just hope it will be quick—perhaps within the year?—and I don't have to kill too many people.

*Another pause.*

I admit that's not what I wanted to hear.

*Even with modern starships, trips into interstellar space were hardly fast. She asked for a destination, and was sent the coordinates of an uncharted system a dozen light‐years away. Unfortunately that was still most of a day.*

*Long years of practice had made Homura skilled at maintaining a kind of passive situational awareness, and even as both ships cruised along, maintaining light stealth, she placed herself in a kind of meditative state, magic suppressing the need for sleep. Suitable for days or weeks of loneliness.*

*So when an alien cruiser dropped out of stealth three‐point‐seven hours later, accompanied by a pair of FTL missiles nearly on top of her, she was able to react in time.*

---

Again there was a very poorly placed timeskip, and now it was obvious Homura's memory was holding something close to its chest. Something about what exact magic Homura was performing. Kyouko would have found the situation Homura was in quite a pickle, and she didn't know Homura to have any kind of teleportation magic, and yet the next scene was on board the ship of the drone magical girls.

Correction: Homura hadn't *originally* had any kind of teleportation magic, but she had used something similar three times the last time Kyouko had seen her, right after New Athens. So, perhaps it wasn't so strange.

---

*Homura stared at where the soul gems had been in her palm. She had tried her best to capture the drone magical girls alive, but somehow the soul gems had burned themselves out, minutes after she had taken them, despite her best attempts to apply stasis.*

*Some kind of soul magic she was unfamiliar with, most likely. Well, the Goddess would show more mercy to them than she would.*

*Homura stepped over the shattered drone bodies and passed her magic over the pilot's command console. A civilian transport normally had nothing in the way of internal defenses, and it seemed her opponents had not added any while making modifications. Nor was there a full onboard AI, and the relatively light security meant that it took remarkably little effort for Homura to commandeer the controls. She was hardly an electronics expert, but she had been forced to pick up a trick or two over the years.*

*There would be time to screen the ship for tracking devices once she got away. For now, the important fact was that the ship's alien stealth device was still active, ironically hiding her just as well from the alien cruiser as the drones had probably intended to hide from her. Her own ship had been destroyed, she had lost her captives, and the drones had reduced their translation device to ashes, but on the whole, she still felt victorious. Finally, a goal was within sight.*

*There was no time to waste. She wouldn't make the same mistakes twice. She had* work.

---

From there the memory transitioned back to a montage of fleeting moments, with just enough detail to make the timeframe clear, obviously intended more to provide context than elucidate anything of depth. Some of it was presumably simple summarization.

Homura tried, initially, to find and extinguish this conspiracy herself, stubbornly ignoring the Goddess's clear suggestions to recruit some help. This took her well outside the usual Human circles, away from the battlefields and into the obscure parts of Human space, tracing every rogue colony known to both Governance and the MSY.

She found some alien supply lines, and work being done on what Kyouko recognized as something similar to the wormhole station destroyed in the Saharan Raid, but no alien home worlds or TCF conspiracy—Utopia Unbound—home bases.

More than once Homura had wondered aloud whether the Goddess was *sure* the war was not already lost and more than once she received an answer indicating… that it was at least not the worst case. On a few occasions Homura intervened when in the area, saving some colonial militia ships, and even personally stopping an attempt to bomb a shipyard. But she knew it was pinpricks relative to the scale of the war, and when she intervened, it was more of a personal gesture, to make herself feel better.

At points Homura seemed to consider recruiting a follower, someone to work with her, as the Goddess was clearly implying was necessary, but she never quite followed through. Her options were limited anyway: a newly‐minted magical girl from a rogue colony would hardly be terribly reliable, and this didn't seem the time to pull anyone away from front‐line combat. Nor was Homura interested in making contact with anyone from her past life.

Then the narrative reached a point that got Kyouko's clear attention: Homura pulling into orbit of New Athens on the anniversary of the day she had saved the planet. On that day, down on the surface, Kyouko had given the most important speech of her life.

---

This hardly seems like a fertile place to find useful agents, *Homura thought, addressing the void.*

Okay yes, there are a lot of magical girls here, but what, do you want me to walk up to one of them and just talk to them? No, don't answer that, of course you do, but I'm not going to risk revealing myself so close to Kyouko of all people—yes, same planet is plenty close, in my opinion. Why do you think anyone would even believe me? Obviously I'm just a crazy Ancient talking to a ribbon in my hair.

*There was a long pause.*

You spoke to *Kyouko?* I thought you were always against revealing yourself to more people. Just to give me a base of recruitment?

I've always admired the versatility of your acts.

*There was another pause, and then Homura sighed.*

You really think they'll listen?

---

Kyouko couldn't help but feel a shiver, unrelated to the cold of Yenisei.

It was one thing to accept intellectually that your life, and your recent upending of it, had been part of the plan of a greater power. It was another to see parts of *your* plan baldly sketched out, in a manner that didn't seem to leave much room for your agency.

She had indeed had her own talk with the Goddess. The Goddess had never told her in plain terms to found the Church, or even to recruit followers. She had actually gotten the impression the Goddess was very reluctant on the matter. Kyouko had offered her ideas, arguing that magical girls needed, more than ever, the hope that Homura's secret could provide. So many girls were being thrown into relentless, bereaving combat—it was like the old days, before the MSY. Even if no one believed her, she had to at least try to offer them comfort.

The Goddess had never even explicitly agreed, implying only that She wouldn't try to stop her. Kyouko had even felt guilty about ignoring Her apparent wishes.

*That* was the understanding with which she had poured out her heart at New Athens. Knowing the Goddess a bit better after a couple decades, it did not surprise her to learn that She held more than one perspective on the matter, and that there was some degree to which her choices weren't her own. But emotionally, she couldn't help but feel manipulated.

That *was* a sensation she hadn't felt in a long time. Even Homura and Yuma had usually avoided leaving her in the lurch.

A disturbing realization then crawled over her: this was surely how Ryouko complained about feeling in the reports. How *Maki* felt, about Kyouko's interventions in her life.

A thought she had to file away for consideration later, probably with a lot more food on hand. For now she had to swallow her feelings and press onward.

---

I should have known you had something like this up your sleeve, *Homura thought, once the speech was over and there had been time to look at the local net's reaction. Of course, a few minutes was far too short to* really *know if the fire would spread, but she could easily read the winds.*

*Really, she should have known* Kyouko *had it in her. That was a real blind spot—after so many centuries, she had never really expected any of them to feel Her again in this life, even with one of Her ribbons. But it made sense, because the Goddess tended to only kick pebbles when She intervened. Rarely ever boulders.*

*Well, to be more precise,* she *was the boulder. At least when Clarisse van Rossum wasn't serving the role.*

*But whatever success Kyouko might have soon, Homura was still reluctant to make direct use of it. Sure, she could see the value in a recruiting pool that was conditioned to take her word on the Goddess and the nature of her mission, but that solved only one of a succession of problems.*

*She took a few seconds to mull that over, and then her ship notified her of an incoming transmission.*

*To say that was unexpected was an understatement. Homura knew from experience that the alien stealth device she was using was very, very good, and should have kept her hidden in orbit from all but magical clairvoyants, none of whom should have had any reason to look for her up here.*

*There was only one possible chink in her armor: to listen to the broadcast she had been obliged to request a tightbeam signal, which was an obvious risk, except that Homura could easily perform the minor bit of magical hacking necessary to spoof a legitimate request. She had even spent the past few months refining her techniques.*

*Not enough, apparently.*

*The message was itself by tightbeam, originating from a way station on the surface. The authentication claimed it was from someone she knew: Jeannette Smith, on behalf of her wife Yasuhiro Rin.*

*Homura sighed. Of course. She* knew *she'd been too sentimental, coming back here.*

*For a moment she considered simply fleeing, but abandoned the thought—if Rin and Jeannette wanted to expose her, they could have done so already. Her best chance of preventing that was to simply talk them out of it. In fact, that was all the message requested: a chat.*

*As for how that damned couple knew it was Akemi Homura they were addressing—well, being infuriatingly perceptive was Rin's special talent.*

*She sighed and dictated a response: she would meet with them, if they agreed to travel to a particular location in deep space, where Homura wouldn't be observed decloaking.*

---

*It turned out, Rin and Jeannette contacting her was a blessing in disguise. They had repeated Rin's Unification Wars disappearance trick, picking up new identities and lives to look for a worthy cause, and eagerly signed on to work with her. And frankly, Homura couldn't have picked better candidates than a magical girl detective and an electronics specialist.*

*It had worked so well, that is, that it carried the clear whiff of higher planning. 'Blessing' indeed.*

*Homura had never been averse to looking even a divine horse in the mouth, but this time, after an indecent amount of nagging on her part, she received an unanswerable reply: the expansion of the newly‐established Church of Hope had another hidden purpose, one which mirrored a purpose Homura's own little conspiracy would have. The 'Laplace's Demon' that the enemy had mentioned was a serious threat, and the Goddess intended that Her two ribbons would cover its eyes. She did not like exerting influence upon Humanity, but Her divine intervention was necessary to bind the Demon's power. Given that tension, She preferred having as many pebbles as possible to nudge.*

*That, and one or three boulders, of course.*

---

To say that this revelation would be explosive within the Church was putting it rather mildly. The Church knew quite well that the Goddess had mixed feelings about its existence, but *why* they were allowed to worship Her now had always been controversial. The common answer, promoted by Kyouko herself, was that they were necessary to support magical girl morale and war mobilization, that they were a beacon of hope in the darkness. Homura's version was… much less inspirational.

Like so much here, Kyouko had to put that into a mental box to think about later.

---

*When Homura relayed that information to Rin, it brought a new revelation.*

*Rin and Jeannette hadn't abandoned the flailing war effort just to fulfill some wanderlust—they had, even prior to the war, begun to grow suspicious of something slightly wobbly in the ever‐turning wheels of Governance, some kind of ineffable magical influence that lay at the center of several anomalies she had tried to track. That the war suddenly forced the MSY to enter Governance's fold made them even more suspicious. But after nearly a year of effort, they had failed to derive all but the faintest leads, which had only made Rin* more *certain—nothing eluded her that long without deep magical support.*

*Her original reason for signing on with Homura so quickly had been to check if it was* her—*apparently her third most likely theory. But their brief time together had made clear that wasn't the case. Rather, Homura's information about 'Laplace's Demon', and experience with magical girl drones, explained much. Some kind of magical predictive engine could dodge even Rin's machinations, if it were powerful enough, and if it acted via agents whose existence violated old assumptions.*

*Homura would have liked to be smug about outmaneuvering the legendary Yasuhiro Rin, except she knew damn well the Goddess had been leading her by the nose. Moreover, she didn't like the way Rin was now openly making a project of untangling the Goddess's historical influence on everything that had ever happened.*

*But she liked even less the conclusions Rin began reaching now that she was in the fold. First, that they had to keep recruiting; unfortunately, Homura was now outvoted three to one on the question. But second, as they added new members, Jeannette finally completed the pattern she and Rin had been working on the last year: the conspiracy Homura had been trying to find was* also *responsible for a breach in the TCF.*

*For a while, plumbing the depths of the breach, and what it was being used for, was their highest priority. Their little cabal—which Inoue Yoko eventually dubbed the Coordinating Group—spread itself thin across the stars, tracing the evidence with growing horror. Governance, the MSY, the Armed Forces—all were fatally compromised, with Laplace's Demon directing a surgical program of magical patching and unpatching that evaded all magical audits that might reveal it.*

*Yet so much was inexplicable—not just the age of the digital backdoor, which they traced back to shortly after the Unification Wars, but rather, the mildness of the exploitation. Utopia Unbound appeared to only be cloaking their operations and gathering data!*

*It was Rin who pointed out, though, that even a breach of this scale couldn't be exploited in unlimited fashion, precisely because of the need to constantly dodge magical audits—fatally compromise the war effort, yes; execute a bloodless coup, no.*

*So, apparently, the drones hadn't been lying about the limits to their cooperation with the aliens. But that was little comfort. And now the question was: what to do about it?*

---

*That exact question dominated the debates of their Coordinating Group. The natural answer was to try to coordinate with Governance and the MSY and seal the TCF backdoor all at once; unfortunately, Jeannette's projections indicated that there weren't enough magical girls with the right powers, nor fast enough technological methods, to do it in time. Even a couple hours was more than enough for Utopia Unbound to cripple the war effort.*

*There was a* second *obvious answer: execute a decapitation strike.*

*Some groundwork would be needed: first, they needed to actually* find *Utopia Unbound's leaders, and then they needed a technological solution to block IIC communications, so no command to exploit the TCF breach could go out. No such technology existed yet, but with time they could perhaps induce Governance to develop something for them.*

*This approach was advocated for by several members, and had the appeal of being both complete and seemingly straightforward—something that Homura had long ago learned the value of. After all, as Napoleon had said, when setting out to take Vienna, take Vienna.*

*Unfortunately the checklist of things that could go wrong was simply too long: the leadership might not all be in one place, there might be a dead man's switch, and they had to not see it coming, even with Laplace's Demon.*

*If Utopia Unbound were ever truly endangered, and they realized it, they could sign on fully with the Cephalopods, wringing whatever leverage they could out of the incredible mounds of intel they held, not to mention the Human shipyards and fleets and exotic matter refineries they could sabotage. With the current state of the war, and frankly, in any probable near future state, that was tantamount to offering the aliens victory.*

*Utopia Unbound might not be traitors to that degree* yet, *but if cornered… well, they had already sold out Humanity once.*

*That put Homura's Coordinating Group in a frankly depressing bind, and they spent some weeks thinking through how they might escape.*

*One unpalatable approach was to attempt negotiations with Utopia Unbound. Homura felt that her past encounter with them was more than enough evidence of bad faith in that regard, and besides, no one wanted to contemplate what kinds of concessions might be demanded. Jeannette called it 'surrendering without a fight' and Homura couldn't really disagree.*

*Another suggestion was a form of strategic stalling: wait for a moment in the war where Humanity had enough of a temporary advantage that it would be safe to patch the TCF and undergo the risk of Utopia Unbound sabotage. Better, but hardly easy to calculate or achieve, especially anytime soon.*

*The nature of the war troubled them, too. Governance simulation and standard logic made one conclusion clear: any aggressive alien power ought to be overwhelmingly powerful, enough to overrun Humanity with barely a fight. The statistically likely differences in civilizational size and age made it almost a guarantee. Even magic shouldn't have been enough to bridge the gap, barring heavy divine intervention. That Humanity had held out already for over a year was incredibly implausible a priori.*

*Nonetheless, the explanation for Humanity's continued independence had to lie within that implausible space, and the most obvious explanation was that the aliens were not fully invested into the war. That was even consistent with what the Utopia Unbound drones had told Homura, and the apparent internal conflict among the aliens.*

*But, frankly, that assumption wasn't useful on its own. A careless attempt at negotiation with the various alien factions could easily backfire—it certainly didn't seem like Utopia Unbound was pleased with the results of* their *negotiations. Though that didn't mean they couldn't try.*

*For now, only one clear path presented itself: to lead their enemies into a deep, twilit forest, and then strangle them there. It had to be over before they saw it coming. To accomplish that against a currently superior foe, whose strength lay in predicting the future…*

*Someone had done that once—alone, even—but Yuma had faced opponents with much more serious restraints, and a precognitive who might very well have let her win. Lacking such advantages, their counterplot would have to be so exquisitely precise, yet so devilishly insidious, that even Laplace's Demon could never anticipate it.*

*Fortunately, that* was *possible. Unfortunately, Homura knew exactly from Whom they might obtain such a plot.*

---

*Homura squinted at the sheet of metal in her hands, laser‐etched to atomic precision with row after row of inscrutable petroglyphs, each a pattern of four symbols arranged within a circle. It might have been mistaken for a lost ancient language, but there was something about the curves involved, the connections between the quarters, that suggested something ineffably alien.*

*Or so she liked to imagine. The atomic precision was, of course, pointless, since she had designed the etchings entirely from memory, based on an image the Goddess had cast into her mind. It was, She claimed, a message in an alien language, one that would help convince a friendly alien faction of her goodwill.*

*Only help. The friendly aliens' previous five attempts to make independent contact with Humanity had been sabotaged by hostile activity, either from compromised Governance AIs, Utopia Unbound agents, or false flag attacks from the attacker faction. They were on the verge of giving up, and after repeated hostile receptions, they weren't likely to believe her claims on their face.*

*The question then was obvious. Why was she being given a single document to hand over instead of, say, a translation guide to the alien written language, or even better, a way to get an enchanted translation device like Utopia Unbound had? At the least, she could take an enormous box of grief cubes and sojourn with the friendly aliens, spend however long it took to build up a common language, even hash things out via hand signals if she had to.*

*Patience, she had been told. The document was efficient, limiting the extent of Her meddling. Full mutual understanding, enough to enchant a translation device or properly communicate, required a certain sacrifice. Moreover, and more importantly, limiting contact with these aliens for now would produce a better final outcome—a proper diplomatic exchange too early would lead to dire consequences, barring extreme interventions on Her part.*

*Would this be like the Unification Wars? Homura had asked. Would She mostly let this war play out, too, acting only to help prevent the worst possible outcomes?*

*That question had caused only sullen silence. She didn't like discussing the topic.*

*The Goddess had never wanted to stand athwart history. Never mind that her power was limited, she had no right to dictate people's choices in life—Her succor came only at death. Nonetheless, her good nature led her to often steal from mortals the worst possibilities, and she felt fewer scruples about adjusting Humanity's course as a whole. She framed it as guiding everyone toward their own, better impulses. She tried not to interfere in anyone's clear, reasoned choices.*

*But, even knowing that, Homura and her Goddess hadn't spoken much, in the latter years of the Unification Wars. They had both felt guilty, and not without good reason. Yet in those days, Homura had at least been able to take solace in the thought that with a United Front victory, the establishment of a lasting peace, and the founding of Governance, her earthly duties would at last be fulfilled, and she could finally rest.*

*She had been wrong. And then, at New Athens, wrong again. How much hope could she have left?*

*Homura hated to think it, hated to consider the notion, but she felt sure that the Goddess* could *have respected her wishes, could have asked less of her, could have shared more with her. How come the Goddess could accept Miki Sayaka's wish, but not hers?*

*Sometimes, Homura felt that Sayaka must be laughing at her, up there in the timeless aether.*

*Not that she thought the Goddess was lying about the big picture, about whatever stopped her from just giving the friendly aliens, Homura, and Humanity what they thought they wanted. Oh no, on that, they were aligned. She was well aware that sometimes it was necessary to take some losses in the short‐term to improve the long‐term. Perhaps even serious losses. That the Goddess didn't like talking about it just showed she still cared.*

*No, it was ultimately about Homura's own, selfish desires. She had a closer relationship to the universe's only divine being than anyone, and still she wanted more. The Goddess was opening up to more magical girls now—couldn't Homura have more, now, too? More magical girls were giving their lives in defense of Humanity than ever before—couldn't Homura, too?*

*And all she had gotten was the same responses, over and over. She supposed when you were timeless there was never really an occasion to compose a better response.*

*Well, for now her only option was to play along. The aliens couldn't be easily communicated with normally, thanks to an incompatible telepathic interface and alien mindset. The block of hieroglyphs in her hand was all she had to work with, unless the Goddess chose to give her more.*

*Even setting up this meeting had required retrocausal information, which taxed the Goddess's resources. Homura had piloted a ship in personally to receive the transmission—a rudimentary encoding of the local star map with location circled—and echo it back, intended as an affirmation. According to her special source, this would prevent the same transmission being picked up by a Governance deep space probe and subsequently diverted by Utopia Unbound.*

*And now she was here, orbiting around a silent world roaming the interstellar void just inside the boundary of the Nile Sector. Without divine guidance she would have been very nervous about her position, alone and plainly uncloaked in a civilian‐class ship that could be blown up at a whim.*

*At least she had gotten very used to this kind of travel, filled as it was with lonely views of a dark horizon far from any of her kind. Or even the Incubators, for that matter—she was hiding from them, too. She had learned the technique from an ex‐SMC soul mage some centuries prior, before she died on mission in Pakistan. It was one of a few cards she had kept close to her chest all this time, withheld until she needed it most.*

*She almost jumped when the sensors registered an unknown ship inbound to the system at FTL speeds. This happened more than she liked to admit: her mind, sitting on idle, letting too much time slip away.*

*It only took a few more seconds for her ship to process the finer details of the sensor profile and lack of human transponder signal and conclude that, yes, this had a 98% chance of being an alien vessel. No attempt was being made at any kind of stealthy approach—if anything, they were* trying *to be seen.*

*All of which boded well, of course, even if she felt a tinge of nervousness about the possibility of such a ship triggering one of Governance's deep space border nets. Surely they were remote enough here?*

*She had to take entirely too much on faith nowadays.*

*At this range, it would still be dozens of minutes before they would arrive at the planet, so Homura sent a signal in the clear: a ping with no informational content, meant only to be acknowledged.*

*The reply came back almost immediately, disregarding travel time: a repeat of the ping with the same characteristics, clearly not a natural reflection.*

*It was difficult not to be impatient as the minutes passed, and Homura found herself fiddling with her hair, knowing full well that the aliens didn't care a single whit what her hair looked like. Like Kyouko, she had never allowed Governance hair implants on her scalp. Hair care was simply part of her centuries‐old routine, and she hadn't wanted to lose it.*

*She remembered… no, it was better not to think about that right now.*

*At length the alien ship grew near, and it began to fire a repeated infrared beam at a location on the planet's surface. Combined with the trajectory, the meaning was clear: they intended to land, and intended her to follow.*

*She had anticipated this. Docking procedures in space were hard enough without needing to deal with alien locking clamps and airlocks, and potential hostility and ambushes. Dropping to the surface made them both a bit more vulnerable as well, but it didn't bother her much: she certainly had no intention of attacking her alien guests, and she knew there was no way the Goddess would let her drop into danger here.*

*Well, perhaps a little danger, but never any real risk.*

*She was becoming a little* too *grumpy with the Goddess, she chided herself. Here, at least, she was doing something quite real. And it wasn't like the planet's surface was a vacation destination—without the light of a star, the only question about most rogue planet surfaces was what flavor of frozen gas or rock you would be standing on, and how many dozens of Kelvin the ambient temperature would be, which affected how long your environmental suit would last.*

*Really, trying to coordinate a meet‐up there was only moderately easier than it had been in space. That the aliens had chosen a place like this showed what their mindset was like after the previous disasters.*

*She had come prepared for this possibility, of course, as well as the possibility of needing to attempt a docking, or make a spacewalk. Thus, she was already wearing the right underlayers and could step right into the necessary ultra‐low temperature environmental suit.*

*As the ship lowered itself toward the surface—specifically, a wide stretch of shallow ice plains only a few centimeters deep—she picked up her message‐engraved plate, vacuum‐sealed into a protective sleeve. If necessary, she had prepared backups, and could even shine the message through a special filter on her flashlight.*

*It took only a few seconds for the ship's thrusters to sublimate the ice below, and then she stepped out onto the bare rock. A surface was a surface, no matter how frozen.*

*Homura pulled out a pair of magically‐enhanced farsight goggles she had cribbed from the MSY, a pair that dated back to, of all people, Mikuni Oriko.*

*It turned out she had hardly needed to bother. The Cephalopods were already waiting, a pair of them standing in plain view a hundred meters away in their own suits, both of them large and barrel‐shaped, far larger than Homura, or indeed any of the numerous alien combat troops she had killed in her time.*

*That alone was strange. She had seen on the battlefield that the aliens had a diversity of combat or computing augments, but this was on a whole different level.*

*Still, sensor scans showed they carried no apparent weaponry, though the very nature of the environmental suits meant that they leaked very little informative radiation.*

*One of the aliens moved aside as she approached, lifting one of several arms to gesture at a glowing panel on the side of their ship. The message was obvious: they wanted her to board.*

*It was a big ask, in a way, since aboard the alien ship was hardly neutral ground, but Homura could hardly blame them after their previous encounters with humans. And as before, she herself had little to fear.*

*She hoped the Goddess's implied protection wasn't making her too reckless.*

*She had prepared as well as she could, but it was still an understatement to say she had no idea what to expect. She had dictated all kinds of contingency plans for various outré scenarios to her cortical implants, fully expecting that she would have to play everything by ear regardless.*

*As someone had said once, plans were useless but planning was everything.*

*She jumped up towards the alien ship, barely catching herself on the side of the door when it slid open just a bit too slow. Right. She was showing a bit too much power.*

*She felt a tad naked taking off her environmental suit in the forcefield airlock, all too aware that even with her magic, being ejected back into the near‐vacuum outside would be unpleasant and annoying, especially were she slow to get a bubble up.*

*Then she raised an eyebrow when she stepped inside the alien ship proper, making a special note in her memory logs to review everything she saw later. It was simply* different *from the alien ships she had seen before, in ways both subtle and not, supporting the Goddess's claim that they represented a different alien faction. In particular, there were a number of strange thin metal rods embedded throughout the room—sensors of some kind, presumably.*

*Overall, though, she was a bit relieved to see that she was looking at a scenario three: in front of her was an obvious conference table, complete with two trios of seats facing each other, with the seats on one side obviously more suited for humans.*

*For now, though, the room was empty.*

*She pulled a scanner out of her pocket and waved it over the room, checking it with her magic at the same time. Hopefully the aliens wouldn't mind a little due diligence, as long as she carried it out as visibly as possible.*

*That was when the oversized door at the far side of the room irised open, admitting another Cephalopod that was as grossly oversized as the ones outside. Without the suit, her scanners could read more clearly what was going on—as best as her sensors could tell, this alien had a prodigious amount of implants in its core, enough to require a dedicated heat radiator in the back. But it didn't look like weaponry—if anything, it looked like computing. They really had mastered biotechnology, it seemed.*

*The alien sat into one of the comically oversized seats on the other side of the table, bending down with a grace that she wouldn't have given them credit for. Then it gestured with a large arm‐tentacle toward one of the human chairs. The message was obvious.*

*Homura couldn't help but look over the chair one last time—it rather resembled a piece of custom‐molded plastic—before taking her seat. It shifted to her presence, like many of the modern human chairs, but was just a little off, poking her in one of her lumbar vertebrae.*

*As she did, the door to the airlock once again slid open, and the two aliens that had been outside stepped in single‐file. Part of Homura wanted to remain on edge, tense against the possibility that sitting her down had been a trap to render her less mobile. She had to actively calm herself, and realized, with an almost perverse sense of relief, that the Goddess's protections had indeed not dulled her sense of danger.*

*The other two aliens took their seats, making clear that Humanity was a bit underrepresented here relative to their expectations. Homura wondered if she should wait: the aliens probably had some attempted communication scheme cooked up.*

*She decided to take the initiative. The Goddess's pictogram message should be self‐explanatory, and would skip a lot of potentially unnecessary throat‐clearing about who had what motives.*

*She pulled the metal slab out of the sleeve on the side of her garment, holding it up for a moment for everyone to look at, before setting it on the table, where it was bombarded by the inevitable barrage of sensor scans, one of the aliens leaning ponderously over.*

*Once that activity had calmed down, she reached her hand out and pushed it forward, though by now the aliens should have spotted the quite clear inscriptions—and judging by the tentacle gestures, they had, or so she imagined.*

*The one in the middle picked it up immediately, human‐like eyes casting over the surface.*

*The reaction after that was almost comical, the three aliens squeezing together to peer at the piece of metal barely wider than one of their arm tentacles. She doubted they lacked the human ability to simply take a picture of the slab and sit back into their chairs to study it in their heads, but they preferred to study it in front of them. Very human, in a way.*

*At brief intervals they exchanged glances, and the occasional twitching arm wave showed what was going through their minds. With a reaction like this, there was no doubt that they understood the slab, as the Goddess had promised.*

*Not that she had ever doubted.*

*She knew what it said, of course, approximately.
